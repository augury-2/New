## PART 3 — The insertable material

### BLOCK A — §1 Introduction (121 words)

*Insert immediately after the paragraph beginning "In this context, branding is distinct from website or app branding in four ways…"*

> These four attributes do not operate additively. Persistence sustains relationships beyond a single visit, embodiment ties consumption to identity work, synchronous sociality supplies the audience that makes identity claims meaningful, and co-creation converts audiences into contributors. Because each attribute conditions the others, the marginal contribution of any one of them depends on which of the remaining attributes are present, a dependence that variance-based models absorb into an average net effect and therefore obscure. Configurational reasoning treats this interdependence as the object of analysis rather than as residual variance, asking which bundles of conditions are sufficient for high brand success. This study accordingly shifts the question from how much engagement, experience and satisfaction matter to when, and in what combination, they matter.

---

### BLOCK B — §2 Literature review, new subsection (166 words)

*Insert as a new numbered subsection after §2.4, before §3.*

> **2.5 From linear antecedents to configurational recipes**
>
> Engagement research has accumulated largely through variance-based designs that estimate the average net effect of each antecedent while holding the others constant (Brodie et al., 2011; Jeong and Kim, 2019; Ebrahim, 2020). This tradition has established that engagement, experience and satisfaction each matter, but it carries three assumptions that immersive settings strain. The first is symmetry, whereby the conditions producing high brand success are taken to be the mirror image of those producing low success. The second is unifinality, whereby one dominant path is presumed to exist. The third is additivity, whereby antecedents are treated as separable rather than mutually constitutive (Schneider and Wagemann, 2012; Douglas et al., 2020). Metaverse branding strains all three, because avatar-mediated identity work, persistent community and co-creation make the value of any single condition contingent on its companions (Hennig-Thurau et al., 2023; Bilgihan et al., 2024). Set-theoretic methods relax these assumptions and are therefore appropriate where causal complexity, rather than incremental variance, is the phenomenon of interest (Ragin, 2008; Jia et al., 2022).

### BLOCK C — §3.2 Theoretical foundation (112 words)

*Append to the end of §3.2, after the paragraph beginning "The two theories are complementary…"*

> This complementarity carries a configurational implication. Flow is a threshold state rather than a linear accumulation: absorption occurs once challenge and skill are matched, and further increments in either contribute little (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990). SDT likewise treats its three needs as partially substitutable in their contribution to motivation, since a deficit in relatedness can be offset by strong autonomy and competence (Ryan and Deci, 2017). A theory of thresholds combined with a theory of substitutable needs predicts equifinality rather than a single dominant pathway. The set-theoretic design therefore follows from the theoretical frame rather than being imposed upon it, and Proposition P1 is derived from Flow Theory and SDT rather than merely being compatible with them.

### BLOCK E — §6 Discussion, new opening subsection (206 words)

*Insert directly after the §6 opening sentence ("The findings of the fsQCA explain how the interplay of UE, UX and BSAT…"), as a new §6.1, before the existing per-configuration paragraphs. This is the highest-value block in the pack.*

> **6.1 Why the paths are not interchangeable**
>
> The three sufficient paths differ in what they demand of a brand, and reading them together clarifies where the study departs from linear accounts.
>
> BSAT × UX describes a curated route in which satisfaction is sustained by experience quality rather than by participation. It is the least resource-intensive path for brands entering immersive channels, because value accrues from design and reliability rather than from cultivating an active community. BSAT × UE describes a relational route in which participation supplies the raw material and satisfaction converts it into advocacy, echoing evidence that engagement translates into loyalty only where the evaluative judgement is favourable (Jeong and Kim, 2019; Ebrahim, 2020). UX × UE is theoretically the most instructive path, because brand success arises without high reported satisfaction. Flow offers an explanation: absorption is intrinsically rewarding and is experienced pre-reflectively, so users deeply involved in a challenge–skill-matched environment may never form or report a discrete satisfaction judgement while nonetheless continuing, purchasing and advocating (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990; Shin, 2019; Zhong and Hamouda, 2024). Satisfaction, on this reading, is the dominant but not the indispensable conduit, a conclusion unavailable to a mediation model, which by construction requires the mediator to carry the effect.

**Note:** Block E makes the paper's title (*"Satisfaction as the Conduit"*) defensible. At present the title claims a conduit role that the UX × UE configuration partially contradicts, and nothing in the manuscript reconciles the two. This block does that reconciliation, which is worth flagging in your response-to-reviewers letter.

### BLOCK F — §8 Limitations and future scope (74 words)

*Insert after the paragraph beginning "The cross-sectional design cannot track how engagement and experience evolve…"*

> Two further boundaries follow from the design. First, fsQCA establishes set-theoretic sufficiency rather than causal identification, so the configurations describe which combinations are consistently associated with high brand success, and experimental or longitudinal designs are required to establish direction. Second, the analysis is asymmetric by construction, so the recipes for high brand success cannot be inverted to explain low brand success; analysing the negated outcome would constitute a distinct and worthwhile study (Schneider and Wagemann, 2012).
