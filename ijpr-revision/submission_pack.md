# 0. Read this first: the one issue that outranks everything else

The original manuscript states in Section 1 that **"the model is specified here, not estimated, and no empirical results are reported."** The original abstract nevertheless states that **"The empirical analysis demonstrates that circular accounting capability significantly enhances information transparency"** and that **"value-chain governance acts as a critical conditional enabler."**

Those are results that appear nowhere in the paper. There is no sample, no measurement model, no path coefficients, no *R²*, no bootstrap intervals. The only data in the manuscript are the three rounds of expert content-validity ratings (S-CVI/Ave 0.799 → 0.908) and the arithmetic in Table 3.

This has been corrected rather than propagated. The revised abstract, contributions and discussion describe the study as what it is: construct development, instrument validation, formal specification analysis, and a pre-registrable protocol. **No statistical output has been invented anywhere in the revision.** If the abstract had been left as written, a desk editor at *IJPR* would very likely reject the submission on integrity grounds, and a reviewer who noticed the contradiction would say so in terms that are hard to recover from. Section 9 of this pack sets out the options for the empirical-status problem, which remains the single largest barrier to acceptance.

# 2. Step 2 — How the study was repositioned without changing it

The repositioning rests on four moves, none of which alters a construct, a path, a sign, an item or a statistic.

**Move 1 — the carbon bridge.** An inter-organisational material loss is also an embodied-emissions loss, and it is unrecorded for the same reason: no entity recognises it. Net-zero manufacturing targets are set on chain-level quantities while accounting is bounded by the legal entity, which is precisely the structural gap the paper already identified. This converts the paper's existing argument into a net-zero argument without adding a variable.

**Move 2 — accounting as the calculative layer of smart production.** The paper already positions accounting as the "sensing apparatus of circularity." Recast against the smart-production literature, this says that digital technologies generate *data* while accounting generates *decision objects*, and that the field has been estimating a three-stage mechanism (instrumentation → calculation → accountable action) as a one-stage one. This is the paper's most distinctive contribution to the Special Issue and it required no model change — only the promotion of the existing "digital traceability capability" control from an unexplained variable to a theorised rival explanation.

**Move 3 — the setting as manufacturing.** Fruit processing is manufacturing. The processors, juice and concentrate lines, peel and pomace valorisers and Common Facility Centres in the studied network are production units, and they are the tier the sampling design deliberately over-samples. Production research already treats comparable settings as manufacturing systems, and three such precedents are now cited.

**Move 4 — governance as the socio-technical conversion condition.** The existing focal hypothesis is, restated, that intervening on the technical subsystem alone will not deliver reconfiguration because the conversion is performed by the social subsystem. This is a standard socio-technical prediction that the operations literature has largely made qualitatively; the paper gives it a testable form. Adding socio-technical systems theory as a framing complement therefore strengthens the theory section without disturbing the dynamic-capabilities core.

# 4. Step 4 — Revised abstract (288 words)

Net-zero manufacturing is a commitment expressed in quantities, yet the decisive share of those quantities arises outside the reporting boundary of the firm that commits to them. Circular economy strategies and smart production technologies are the principal levers for closing that distance, but neither is self-executing: digital instrumentation generates data, and data becomes a decision object only once it is quantified, costed and valued, while a valued opportunity becomes realised material re-routing only where some actor is answerable for the loss. In fragmented manufacturing networks of dispersed small producers, informal intermediaries and specialised processors, conventional management accounting recognises purchased inputs and saleable outputs while the fraction leaving a production process as waste is recognised only as an absence. This study operationalises circular accounting capability as a measurable, actor-level construct spanning organisational boundaries, and specifies a moderated serial-mediation model in which the capability raises circular information transparency, transparency enables circular value-chain integration conditional on circular governance quality, and integration delivers economic, environmental and social circular value creation. The framework combines the dynamic capabilities view with value-chain governance theory and a socio-technical reading of smart production, treating governance as the complementary asset that substitutes for hierarchy where the value-creating reconfiguration crosses firm boundaries. Three rounds of expert validation with an eight-member panel raised scale-level content validity from 0.799 to 0.908 and established the construct's discriminant boundaries, with leakage between the accounting–transparency and governance–integration pairs of 4.2 and 2.1 per cent against a pre-specified 20 per cent tolerance. Formal specification analysis rejects the intuitive four-link serial chain, whose indirect effect falls below 0.03 once path coefficients drop under 0.40. The paper delivers a validated instrument and a pre-registrable estimation protocol for the measurement and accountability infrastructure on which industrial decarbonisation depends.

## 9.1 Remaining weaknesses, in order of how likely each is to cause rejection

**1. The paper reports no structural results. (Severity: critical.)**
*IJPR* is an empirical and analytical journal. It publishes surveys, models, simulations, case studies and reviews. It very rarely publishes research protocols, and a Special Issue titled "From Vision to Practice" invites a reviewer to observe that the paper does not reach practice. Three options:

- **(a) Field the survey first — strongly recommended.** The instrument is validated and the protocol is written. With *n* ≥ 350 the paper becomes a conventional empirical submission with an unusually strong measurement pedigree and a genuinely novel construct. This is the option most likely to result in acceptance.
- **(b) Submit as a scale-development paper.** Reframe the contribution as instrument development, with the structural model presented as the nomological network to be tested. This requires foregrounding §5.5, adding a pilot EFA/CFA on even 120–150 responses, and retitling toward "development and validation of a measure of…". Publishable, but the interesting claim (the governance contingency) becomes a promise.
- **(c) Submit as a conceptual/position paper.** Lowest probability of success at *IJPR*. If pursued, target a journal that publishes theory papers in this space and keep *IJPR* for the empirical follow-up.

If (a) is impossible before the deadline, an enquiry to the guest editors describing the paper's status precisely is worth more than a speculative submission.

**2. Length. (Severity: high.)** The revised body is ~14,500 words excluding tables and references; *IJPR* expects roughly 8,000–10,000. A concrete reduction plan to ~10,000:
- §2 literature review: −1,800. It is now comprehensive to the point of being a review. Compress §2.4 and §2.5 into single tighter subsections and move the enumerated citation lists into Table 1.
- §9 managerial implications: −700. Seven audiences is generous; merge to four (production/operations; sustainability and ESG; lead manufacturers and intermediary bodies; technology providers).
- §10 and §11: −800 combined. Merge the SDG subsection into a short paragraph; cut the CBAM subsection if the target readership is not EU-facing.
- §12: −400. The six research directions can be three sentences each rather than a paragraph each.
- §7: −300. The three convergences can be compressed once the literature review is shorter.
- §1: −300. The roadmap paragraph can be two sentences.

**3. Reference count. (Severity: moderate.)** 155 references is high even for a paper with a large literature base. Trim to ~115–120 by removing the second-order citations added for breadth in §2.4 and §2.5 (each subsection currently carries 8–12 supporting citations where 4–6 would do). Every added reference has been Crossref-verified, but **verify each against the publisher record before submission**: several are "in press" articles whose volume, issue and page numbers will have been assigned by the time you submit, and a handful have 2026 online-first dates that may shift.

**4. The setting still requires the reviewer to accept a premise. (Severity: moderate.)** Fruit processing is manufacturing, and the revision argues this with precedents. But a reviewer whose mental model of *IJPR* is discrete-parts manufacturing may still resist. Two strengthening moves: (i) report the number and size distribution of the processing units and Common Facility Centres in the sampling frame, which makes the manufacturing tier concrete rather than asserted; (ii) add one paragraph in §5.2 explaining the process technology involved (grading, extraction, pasteurisation, concentration, peel and pomace processing) so that the production system is visible as a production system.

**5. The construct's antecedent is the weakest scale. (Severity: moderate.)** CAC sits at 0.889 against a 0.90 criterion, and it is the model's antecedent. The revision reports this prominently and diagnoses it (the recovery-valuation dimension, where no prior instrument exists). Before fielding, run the cheap fix: revise the three weakest CAC items and re-rate with the same panel. A fourth round costs little and removes the one measurement objection a careful reviewer will certainly raise.

**6. The placement task was not repeated after twelve items were reworded. (Severity: low–moderate.)** Conceded in §5.5 and §12. A single confirmatory placement round with the same eight-member panel closes this and takes days, not months.

**7. Two control items (IS2, IS3) are double-barrelled and below threshold. (Severity: low.)** Fix before fielding; it is a wording change.

**8. The carbon claim is framed, not measured. (Severity: low, because it is conceded.)** See the caveat in §8 of this pack. The risk is only that a reviewer reads the title's "net-zero" and expects emissions data. Mitigate by making the first sentence of §5.1 state plainly that the instrument is denominated in material and monetary units.

**9. Nine hypotheses with no results is a lot of apparatus. (Severity: low.)** If option (b) above is taken, consider presenting H1–H9 as a table plus two paragraphs rather than nine developed arguments, and reallocating the space to the measurement contribution.

**10. Single-authored generative-AI declaration. (Severity: low, but check.)** The declaration is retained. Confirm it matches Taylor & Francis's current AI policy wording and placement requirements for the journal, which differ from Elsevier's.

## 9.2 What is genuinely strong, and should not be edited away

- **The construct is novel and the novelty is defensible.** Inter-organisational circular accounting capability does not exist as a measured construct, and the discriminant work anticipates the objections that matter.
- **The specification analysis is unusual and impressive.** Rejecting the four-link chain with explicit attenuation arithmetic, before collecting data, pre-empts the single most common reviewer suggestion ("why not put governance in the chain?"). Keep Table 3.
- **Reporting a failed validation round is a real methodological virtue.** Round one failed; the cause is disclosed; the design separates composition effects from revision effects. Most papers would have reported round three only. Do not remove this under length pressure.
- **Power was computed against the interaction, not the largest path.** This is the correct choice and it is rare. Keep Table 6.
- **The failure conditions are stated in advance.** §4.4 is the paper's strongest signal of scientific seriousness.
- **The governance-as-conversion argument now explains an actual anomaly** — the inconsistent sign on transparency effects across recent studies. That is a contribution a reviewer can see the value of immediately.
